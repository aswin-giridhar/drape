Three ways to drape

Each section below is a working prototype, not a picture of one. Scroll inside each to feel how it behaves. The photographs are genuine YouCam try-on renders of the same sitter — the garment really is changing on her body.

Recommended 6 real renders

B. Simulated cloth

WebGL / canvas physics

A real cloth mesh — verlet physics, pinned at the top, falling under gravity — tinted by the palette as you scroll. Drag it with your pointer. Striking, but note what it shows: fabric on nobody. It decorates the idea instead of demonstrating it.

C. The swatch curtain

CSS only · no API cost

Palette colours fall across the sitter's face like fabric panels as you scroll — a full-bleed version of the drape panels already in the app. Cheap, quick, tasteful. Also the least memorable: it decorates a portrait rather than answering a question.

True Spring palette

Draped

Each panel is one colour from her measured palette, falling in sequence. Handsome — but it tells you nothing you didn't already know from the swatch row.

Which one, and why

ConceptWhat it demonstratesCost to a judgeRisk
A — Draping scrubThe actual product: her body, her colours, her rankingZero — frames are pre-rendered static filesLow. ~1.5MB of images to preload
B — Simulated clothThat we can write physicsZero, but heavy on low-end devicesHigh. Most build time, least product meaning
C — Swatch curtainA palette, prettilyZeroLow, and low reward

A is the recommendation. It is the only one where the interaction and the product are the same thing: scrolling is draping. B looks the most technical and proves the least. C is a nice flourish that the app already gestures at with its static drape panels.

A and C also compose — the curtain suits the colour-card section, the scrub carries the rail.
